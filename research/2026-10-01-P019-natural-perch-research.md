## 1 · The foot-health issue (owner's angle)

| Finding | Source | Level |
|---|---|---|
| Perches that are all the same size, meaning not enough foot exercise, are named as an important cause of bumblefoot (pododermatitis, sores on the soles) | HARI (Hagen Avicultural Research Institute) bumblefoot article | SNIPPET |
| Wooden dowels are the same diameter and shape along their whole length, so the foot grips the same way on the same pressure points every day. Natural branches vary in shape and thickness, which spreads contact across more of the foot | Parrot Care Central; The Happy Perch care guide | SNIPPET |
| A perch that's too wide stretches the foot; one that's too narrow puts pressure on the wrong part of the foot | HARI | SNIPPET |
| Vet guidance: natural branches of varying diameter promote foot exercise. Avoid dowel, sandpaper and pumice perches | Unusual Pet Vets (AU); AAV "Managing mild bumblefoot" handout | SNIPPET |
| Conures need several perch sizes so they can exercise their feet and avoid pressure sores | petMD / LafeberVet conure sheets | SNIPPET |
| Branches must come from non-toxic, insecticide-free trees | HARI | SNIPPET |

**What we may say in the video:** perches of different sizes and textures, like natural branches, are what bird experts and avian vets recommend, so feet aren't gripping the same spot all day. That's a general care fact with sources.

**What we may NOT say:**
- That *this product* prevents or treats bumblefoot or any disease.
- The listing's "prevent foot disease" claim.
- Disease names as a scare tactic.

The product's role in the video is *variety*, not *cure*. Medical detail goes in the pinned comment, softly, with "ask your avian vet".
